# Federal FBP Fuel Representations: Preliminary Comparison

**Investigation:** D04
**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** Documentary comparison; no raster analysis completed

## Purpose

Determine whether the 2019, 2024, and 2026 national
FBP fuel products can support controlled comparisons
of wildfire exposure around Westwood Hills.

The three products are alternative landscape
representations, not necessarily a consistent
chronological series of vegetation observations.

## Preliminary comparison

| Property | 2019 | 2024 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | CanFG development | FBP 2024 | Current-year FBP |
| Documented resolution | 250 m | 30 m | 30 m default |
| Additional resolution | Unverified | 100 m distribution | 100 m documented |
| Forest evidence | kNN inventory | SCANFI | SCANFI 2.0 |
| Burned-area evidence | Documented | NBAC | Annual updates documented |
| Exact source editions | Incomplete | Unresolved | Incomplete |

| Raster grid alignment | Unverified | Unverified | Unverified |

A 250 m distribution mentioned in the preliminary
2026 note requires independent confirmation.

## Established observations

1. The products have different documented
   spatial resolutions.

2. The source inventory descriptions differ.

3. The 2024 catalogue identifies SCANFI,
   ecozones, and NBAC.

4. The 2026 catalogue explicitly identifies
   SCANFI 2.0 and annual burned-area updates.

5. Common FBP terminology does not establish
   identical numerical codes or assignment rules.

6. The 2023 Westwood Hills fire falls between
   the 2019 and later product editions.

## Distinct sources of disagreement

### Landscape change

Actual vegetation and land-cover conditions may
have changed between source observations.

The 2023 wildfire is a particularly important
potential contributor.

### Source-evidence differences

Different forest inventories, imagery, and
burned-area records may represent different
conditions or observation periods.

### Classification differences

Products may use different decision rules,
category boundaries, or code mappings.

### Spatial-support differences

Raster resolution, cell alignment, and
resampling can change apparent agreement.

These effects must be investigated separately
where technically feasible.

## Planned comparison sequence

1. Verify original code dictionaries.
2. Establish source observation periods.
3. Acquire fixed, identifiable product editions.
4. Define the Westwood Hills analysis boundary.
5. Inspect native raster geometry and coverage.
6. Compare categorical distributions.
7. Examine agreement at controlled spatial support.
8. Investigate the historical fire footprint.
9. Evaluate implications for continuous exposure.
10. Evaluate directional and threshold stability.

These are planned analyses, not completed results.

## Interpretation constraints

Cell-by-cell code agreement is not automatically
agreement in underlying vegetation composition.

Disagreement does not automatically indicate error.

Apparent stability is not a calibrated probability
of correctness or operational safety.

A post-fire map is not necessarily a more accurate
description of current local fuel conditions.

## Priority unresolved questions

- Are the three raster code dictionaries compatible?
- What observation periods underlie each product?
- Were the 2018 decision rules retained or modified?
- Which NBAC editions informed the later products?
- How is the 2023 fire represented in each raster?
- How were coarser distributions generated?
- What common analysis grid is defensible?
