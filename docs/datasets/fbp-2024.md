## D02.2 — Classification comparability

**Review date:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** Partially established

### Confirmed from official metadata

The 2024 dataset uses the Canadian FBP fuel-type
framework and identifies three principal input sources:

- Spatialized Canadian National Forest Inventory (SCANFI)
- Canadian ecozones
- National Burned Area Composite (NBAC)

The dataset catalogue records:

- Publication date: 2024-04-01
- Metadata release date: 2024-07-10
- Temporal coverage: 2020-08-01 to 2024-04-01
- Named 30 m raster distribution: 2024-05-22

These dates do not establish the observation date
of every underlying landscape attribute.

### Comparability with 2019

Both products use FBP fuel-type terminology.

However, common fuel-type terminology does not
establish identical raster code assignments,
classification thresholds, or processing rules.

The 2019 decision-rules documentation cannot yet
be assumed to describe the 2024 classification.

### Outstanding questions

- Does the 2024 product retain the 2019 code mapping?
- Are mixedwood classification intervals unchanged?
- Which SCANFI version and attributes were used?
- Which NBAC edition contributed to the 2024 raster?
- Was the 2023 Westwood Hills footprint included?
- Were new or modified classification rules applied?

### Experimental implication

Compare code dictionaries before measuring
cell-level classification agreement.

Keep classification disagreement separate from
differences in resolution, source observation dates,
and actual landscape change.

## D02.3 — Source evidence and burned-area provenance

**Review date:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** Source families confirmed; versions unresolved

### Confirmed

The official 2024 FBP catalogue identifies three
principal source datasets:

- Spatialized Canadian National Forest Inventory (SCANFI)
- Canadian ecozones
- National Burned Area Composite (NBAC)

The catalogue does not identify the exact SCANFI
or NBAC version used to produce the raster.

### Relationship to newer products

The newer Canadian National FBP Fuel Type Layer
explicitly identifies SCANFI 2.0 and describes
annual incorporation of burned-area information.

This newer documentation does not establish
that the 2024 product used SCANFI 2.0 or the
same annual processing procedure.

### NBAC and the 2023 wildfire

NRCan maintains 2023 NBAC burned-area products.

However, currently distributed 2023 NBAC data
may incorporate revisions made after 2024.

We have not established:

- Which NBAC version informed the 2024 FBP raster.
- Whether the Westwood Hills footprint was included.
- How burned areas were translated into fuel classes.
- Whether affected cells represent post-fire vegetation.

### Experimental implication

We must distinguish:

1. Historical landscape change.
2. Changes in source evidence and its observation dates.
3. Changes in fuel classification procedures.
4. Revisions to retrospective burned-area products.

The 2023 fire footprint should be treated as
contextual evidence until its representation
within the 2024 fuel product is established.
