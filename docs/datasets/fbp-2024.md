## Relationship to the 2019 product

The 2019 product is documented at 250 m.

The 2024 product uses a finer 30 m classification grid.

The products may also differ in source inventory,
burned-area evidence, classification rules, and
landscape conditions.

Classification equivalence is not yet established.

## Westwood Hills considerations

The study-area wildfire occurred in 2023.

The 2024 product may incorporate post-fire information
through its NBAC input.

Whether the relevant 2023 burned area is included,
and how that information affects fuel classification,
requires direct verification.

A difference between the 2019 and 2024 products
must not automatically be interpreted as error.

## D02.2 — Classification comparability

**Review date:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** Partially established

### Confirmed from official metadata

The 2024 dataset uses the Canadian FBP fuel-type
framework and identifies three principal input sources:

- Spatialized Canadian National Forest Inventory (SCANFI)
- Canadian ecozones
- National Burned Area Composite (NBAC)

The dataset catalogue records:

- Publication date: 2024-04-01
- Metadata release date: 2024-07-10
- Temporal coverage: 2020-08-01 to 2024-04-01
- Named 30 m raster distribution: 2024-05-22

These dates do not establish the observation date
of every underlying landscape attribute.

### Comparability with 2019

Both products use FBP fuel-type terminology.

However, common fuel-type terminology does not
establish identical raster code assignments,
classification thresholds, or processing rules.

The 2019 decision-rules documentation cannot yet
be assumed to describe the 2024 classification.

### Outstanding questions

- Does the 2024 product retain the 2019 code mapping?
- Are mixedwood classification intervals unchanged?
- Which SCANFI version and attributes were used?
- Which NBAC edition contributed to the 2024 raster?
- Was the 2023 Westwood Hills footprint included?
- Were new or modified classification rules applied?

### Experimental implication

Compare code dictionaries before measuring
cell-level classification agreement.

Keep classification disagreement separate from
differences in resolution, source observation dates,
and actual landscape change.

## Outstanding verification

- Exact source observation dates and SCANFI version
- NBAC version and treatment of 2023 burns
- Native CRS, extent, and raster alignment
- Complete fuel-code definitions
- Classification decision rules
- Differences from the 2019 classification methodology
- Whether the 100 m product is derived from the 30 m grid
- Treatment of forest, non-forest, and unavailable areas
- Relationship to the 2026 fuel product

## Research implications

Classification disagreement and spatial-resolution
effects should be measured separately where possible.

The 2024 map should be treated as a landscape
representation constructed from multiple evidence
sources, rather than a uniform 2024 field observation.

Continuous source attributes cannot be inferred
directly from categorical FBP codes.
